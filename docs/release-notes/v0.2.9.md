Find out which app really keeps your GPU busy.

Most apps don't use the GPU under their own name. They hand their windows to WindowServer, which puts the screen together, so their GPU work is counted as WindowServer. That is why Activity Monitor, and Activity+ until now, often show a busy GPU and nothing but WindowServer behind it.

**New**
- **Find the Cause** on the GPU page: Activity+ hides your apps one at a time for a few seconds, measures how far WindowServer's GPU time drops, and shows them again. The result names the app responsible and how much it costs, and what is left for the displays, desktop and menu bar
- Apps that draw through a helper process, such as Steam, are measured as the app you know
- When an app stays quiet after being shown again, the result says so: it was redrawing constantly until it was hidden
- Drops within the measurement's own noise are not named, so a wandering load does not point at the wrong app
- The GPU clock is shown against its maximum (for example 389 MHz of 1.30 GHz), so a high percentage at a low clock reads as what it is

The run asks first, takes about five seconds per app, and every app is shown again at the end, also when you cancel or quit.

Requires macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon. Notarized by Apple.
